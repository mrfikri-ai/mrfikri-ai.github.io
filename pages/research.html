Long-Duration Persistent Monitoring

Long-duration monitoring requires autonomous systems to maintain reliable spatial coverage over extended periods while operating under limited energy, sensing, communication, and computational resources. Unlike short-term coverage missions, persistent operation introduces additional challenges such as task redistribution, charging and re-entry, changing environmental priorities, congestion, and the need to preserve monitoring performance despite agents temporarily leaving the mission.

These problems become increasingly difficult as the number of agents grows and as sensing objectives, safety constraints, and shared resources become coupled. A central research question is therefore how a network of autonomous agents can sustain effective monitoring performance over long time horizons while adapting to operational constraints and changes in mission demand.

Here, we show several of our research directions on long-duration persistent monitoring, including coverage, task allocation, resilient coordination, and multi-agent path planning.

02

Resilient Control, Estimation, and Learning for Autonomous and Networked Systems

Modern autonomous systems increasingly operate as interconnected dynamical networks whose performance depends not only on individual controllers, but also on communication, distributed information, estimation, and interactions among agents. Such systems must remain stable and safe despite disturbances, modeling uncertainty, actuator limitations, topology changes, cyber-physical attacks, and imperfect information shared across the network.

At the same time, purely model-based approaches may become difficult to scale to highly nonlinear or uncertain environments, whereas learning-based approaches may lack the stability and safety guarantees required for safety-critical operation. An important challenge is therefore to combine robust control, distributed estimation, optimization, and learning while preserving interpretable performance and safety guarantees at the system level.

Here, we show several of our research directions on resilient networked control, nonlinear control, observer-based estimation, learning-enhanced control, and safety-critical coordination.

Strategic and Belief-Space Pursuit under Partial Information

Autonomous pursuit and interaction problems become fundamentally more difficult when agents have asymmetric or incomplete information about one another. A pursuer may need to infer the position, motion, intention, or future behavior of another agent while simultaneously deciding how to move, acquire information, maintain safety, and accomplish a connection or capture objective.

The problem becomes even richer when multiple agents interact strategically, since the motion of one agent changes the decisions and information available to the others. These scenarios arise in surveillance, search, interception, connectivity maintenance, and autonomous coordination in uncertain environments. The central challenge is to understand how decision making, information acquisition, strategic interaction, and safety can be coupled within a common dynamical framework.

Here, we show several of our research directions on pursuit–evasion, belief-space pursuit, information-aware decision making, and adaptive pursuit under partial information.

04

3D Inspection and Geometric Safety

Autonomous inspection in three-dimensional environments requires vehicles to move close to complex structures while maintaining precise motion, collision avoidance, and physical feasibility. The difficulty increases substantially when the environment itself is dynamic, such as rotating wind-turbine blades, or when obstacle motion is only partially observed and must be predicted from limited sensing.

Unlike planar navigation, aerial inspection must account for full three-dimensional vehicle dynamics, orientation, actuation constraints, moving geometry, uncertainty, and the need to preserve useful inspection trajectories near the structure. These requirements create a challenging interaction between geometric motion planning, prediction, uncertainty, and safety-critical control. A key research question is therefore how autonomous aerial systems can perform close-proximity inspection safely and efficiently when both the vehicle dynamics and surrounding geometry are inherently three-dimensional and time varying.

Here, we show several of our research directions on geometric safety, dynamic-obstacle prediction, uncertainty-aware inspection, and autonomous operation around complex 3D infrastructure.

Ongoing research. Publications and demonstrations will be added as they become available.
